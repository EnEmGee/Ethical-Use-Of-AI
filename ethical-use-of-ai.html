Ethical use of AI

Ethical use of AI means using AI tools in ways that protect human dignity, avoid harm, uphold fairness, respect privacy and support informed decision-making. Ethical use ensures that teachers retain professional responsibility and that AI enhances, rather than replaces, human judgement.

In education, all of this should occur in a broader context in which teachers care for and support the betterment of their students.

The following ACU key principles align strongly with the Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools principles such as fairness, accountability, human and social wellbeing, and transparency.

Text alternative

Human dignity: Students must never be reduced to data or digital likenesses. AI must respect the uniqueness of each person.

Embodiment and relationality: AI cannot replicate presence, empathy or authentic relationship. The teacher–student bond remains central.

Truth and discernment: AI can produce convincing but false outputs. Teachers must help students pursue truth and practise critical judgement.

Stewardship and responsibility: AI is a human creation. Teachers are responsible for its ethical use and for guiding students to use it wisely.

The common good: AI should benefit all learners, not widen inequalities or harm vulnerable communities.
