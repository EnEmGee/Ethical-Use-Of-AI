About this course

Artificial intelligence (AI) is not a mere extension of existing tools but a shift that accelerates how information is produced, evaluated and interacted with, demanding new cognitive and educational responses. It can enhance learning design, support diverse learners, and reduce workload associated with repetitive or highly structured tasks. Yet AI also raises important classroom questions about accuracy, bias, privacy, wellbeing, appropriate dependence and academic integrity.

Course details

What you will learn

By the end of this course, you'll be able to:

  • Apply AI purposefully across common classroom practices, including planning, teaching, assessment, communication, creation and student engagement.
  • Exercise informed professional judgement when using AI, ensuring ethical, responsible and context-appropriate decisions guided by national frameworks.
  • Critically review AI-supported outputs to determine their accuracy, clarity, inclusiveness and educational value for K–12 learners.
  • Integrate AI into everyday teaching workflows in ways that support efficiency and sustainability while retaining teacher agency, accountability and responsibility for learning.

Time commitment

This short course should take you around 20 hours to complete. People learn at different rates. The important thing is that you absorb each new piece of information before you move to the next.

Assessment overview

All Points to Teaching (APTT) students who are eligible for credit will receive recognition of prior learning (RPL) towards one Education elective unit in the Bachelor of Education (Secondary) upon successful completion of all five compulsory short courses.

For RPL, you must:

  • engage in all discussion activities to the standard set out in the rubric (50% of total mark)
  • achieve 50% or higher in any online quiz activities (50% of total mark)
  • achieve a pass (50%) or better in the course overall.
