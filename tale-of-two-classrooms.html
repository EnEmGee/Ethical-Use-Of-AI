Activity: A tale of two classrooms

Let's take the AI time machine to 2035 and compare two future classrooms to understand how different uses of AI shape curriculum, pedagogy, assessment and learner experience.

Explore the two future classrooms that you discover in 2035.

Text alternative

Classroom A: High-automation learning

Curriculum

  • Heavy reliance on AI-generated explanations.
  • Reduced teacher-curated conceptual sequences.
  • Content becomes fragmented and surface-level.

Pedagogy

  • Students default to instant AI answers rather than reasoning.
  • Minimal elaboration, practice, or retrieval.
  • Less cognitive effort results in weaker long-term memory.

Assessment

  • AI-assisted products appear fluent but lack conceptual depth.
  • Harder for teachers to determine genuine understanding.
  • Students struggle when AI is removed.

Learner experience

  • High convenience but low engagement.
  • Inflated confidence ('illusion of knowing').
  • Poorer retention and declining independent performance.

Classroom B: Human-centred, AI-augmented learning

Curriculum

  • Explicit teaching of foundational knowledge before AI use.
  • AI used to extend and enrich thinking, not replace it.
  • Stronger conceptual sequencing and coherence.

Pedagogy

  • AI supports practice, scaffolding, elaboration, and differentiation.
  • Teacher prompts metacognition and strategic decision-making.
  • Students develop independence rather than dependence.

Assessment

  • Tasks require reasoning beyond AI’s capabilities.
  • Transparent, critical AI use is encouraged and evaluated.
  • Teacher can reliably see student thinking.

Learner experience

  • Higher autonomy and intrinsic motivation.
  • Better learning transfer and stronger retention.
  • Improved performance on creative and independent tasks.

Reflect

Reflect on the following:

  • Which classroom aligns more closely with your current/aspirational teaching context?
  • What is one change that would help shift a real classroom from Classroom A toward Classroom B? Select the following to reveal some suggestions.
Suggested changes
  • Curriculum: Teacher connects curriculum content with school, community and real-world contexts.
  • Pedagogy: Students critically review AI answers. For example, students examine an AI-generated historical account for bias using primary sources.
  • Assessment: Students show their thinking. For example, students evaluate an AI-generated fitness plan for safety and effectiveness.
  • Learner experience: Students engage in interest-based projects. For example, students design and conduct a science experiment of personal choice, using AI to suggest variables to test.
