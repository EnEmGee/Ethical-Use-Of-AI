Ethical scenarios

Teachers today work in a world where AI can profile, predict, replicate and even impersonate individuals with astonishing ease. These developments demand a new level of ethical awareness: teachers must be able to identify risks, analyse scenarios, protect student dignity, and make informed decisions grounded in professional responsibility.

The following scenarios illustrate the ethical tensions around digital identity, privacy, profiling and human judgement. Consider each one in connection with the Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools.

The avatar teacher

A school purchases an AI avatar product that can speak in your voice, teach multiple year levels, and provide 24/7 personalised feedback. The company offers to 'clone' your face and tone to make the avatar more engaging for students.

Questions

  • Who owns your digital likeness?
  • Can a teacher give informed consent when employment pressure is present?
  • What happens to the avatar if you resign?
  • Which Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools principles apply (e.g. transparency, human and social wellbeing, accountability)?
The chatbot

A Year 8 student tells you they have been talking to a chatbot 'friend' about personal issues. They feel the chatbot understands them better than adults and are unsure whether humans need to be involved.

Questions

  • What risks arise when a student outsources emotional support to an AI?
  • Is the chatbot storing or analysing this data?
  • How should a teacher respond in a way that protects student wellbeing without dismissing their feelings?
  • Which Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools principles apply?
The uploaded assessment

A teacher uploads a student’s assessment draft into a commercial AI tool to generate feedback. They later discover that the tool retains user inputs for future training.

Questions

  • Has the teacher breached confidentiality?
  • Could this data be reconstructed, resurfaced or misused in future systems?
  • How does this relate to Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools principles on privacy, safety and accountability?
Deepfake humour

A group of students uses an AI image tool to generate a humorous deepfake of a classmate. The image spreads on social media and begins to cause reputational harm.

Questions

  • Who is accountable?
  • How should the teacher guide the class in digital citizenship and dignity?
  • What Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools principles apply?
